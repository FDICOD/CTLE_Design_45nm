Note on channel envelope / fz extraction: A

An attempt was made to extract a data-derived zero frequency (fz) by fitting the upper envelope of the ripple peaks and locating a −3 dB rolloff corner. This was inconclusive — the channel's loss envelope does not follow a smooth, monotonic rolloff even across just the peak values (e.g., −16.3 dB at 0.35 GHz vs. −8.6 dB at 1.35 GHz vs. −16.0 dB at 1.69 GHz), indicating the connector transition is dominated by broadband resonance/mismatch behavior rather than smooth skin-effect/dielectric loss in the low-to-mid frequency range. fz was therefore set using the standard rule-of-thumb instead.

Recommendation: stop trying to derive fz numerically from this channel. It's not that your code or method was wrong — the underlying data genuinely doesn't support a clean single-pole fz extraction. Keep fz ≈ 2.2 GHz (your original rule-of-thumb), note in your write-up that this channel's resonance-dominated behavior made a data-derived fz impractical (a legitimate, honest finding), and let the transient eye diagram be your real validation once you build the schematic.

Derivation chain:

ωp2 = 1/(RL·CL)
Peak Gain ≈ gm·RL                          (once CD shorts RD at high frequency)
DC Gain = gm·RL / (1 + gm·RD/2)
ωz = 1/(RD·CD)
ωp1 = (1 + gm·RD/2) / (RD·CD) = ωz·(1 + gm·RD/2)


Step 1 — RL from fp2, CL: RL = 1/(2π × 10 GHz × 27 fF) ≈ 590 Ω

Step 2 — gm from peak gain target: Peak_linear = 10^(9/20) ≈ 2.818 gm = Peak_linear / RL = 2.818 / 590 ≈ 4.78 mS

Step 3 — Id from gm/Id operating point: Id = gm / 9 ≈ 531 µA per branch (Itail ≈ 1.06 mA total)

Step 4 — W from current density: W = 531 µA / 69.75 µA/µm ≈ 7.6 µm

Step 5 — swing check: Id·RL ≈ 313 mV single-ended (~626 mV differential)

Step 6 — RD (design/half-circuit value) from DC gain target: DC_linear = 10^(2/20) ≈ 1.259 1.259 = 2.818 / (1 + gm·RD/2) → gm·RD ≈ 2.476 RD = 2.476 / 4.78 mS ≈ 518 Ω

Step 7 — CD (design/half-circuit value) from fz: CD = 1/(2π × 2.2 GHz × 518 Ω) ≈ 140 fF

Step 8 — fp1 (falls out, not directly chosen): fp1 = 2.2 GHz × 2.238 ≈ 4.92 GHz

(Notably: fp1 ≈ 4.92 GHz lands below Nyquist, meaning the gain has finished ramping to its full peak before 6.25 GHz — the response delivers its full 9 dB right at Nyquist rather than mid-ramp.)
